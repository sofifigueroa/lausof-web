Experience

More than a decade working in the Puna

Lausof is a family-owned transport and logistics company based in Salta, Argentina. For more than a decade we have been going up to the Puna, the Andean high plateau: we move crews to mine camps, we take cargo and equipment to the salt flats of Salta and Jujuy, and we assist vehicles on the road above 4,000 m (13,000 ft).

Working at altitude is not improvised. The gravel, the weather and the distance are hard on the units and on the people, and a trip that is routine in the city takes planning, equipment and experience up there. That is why we work with units equipped for mine-site entry, drivers who know the road, and an in-house workshop that keeps the fleet ready to go up.

Our licenses, insurance in force and driver certificates are available on request for prequalification.

The road

We know the road and the requirements

We have driven the road from Salta to San Antonio de los Cobres, the Salar de Pocitos and Tolar Grande many times, in every season of the year. We know how it behaves in winter and in summer, where the cell signal drops, and what a unit has to carry to get there and, above all, to get back.

We also know the mine-site entry requirements: units with the equipment demanded at the gate, drivers with a current medical fitness certificate, and paperwork in order. When one of our units reaches the checkpoint, it arrives with what it will be asked for.

Frequently asked questions

What buyers ask before we quote

Do you handle crew changes?

Yes. We move crews between Salta and the camps in 19-seat minibuses, on fixed routes, rotating shifts or one-off trips, depending on how each project works.

Are the units equipped for mine-site entry?

Yes. The 4x4 pickups assigned to those jobs carry a roll cage, a buggy whip, a strobe beacon, fire extinguishers, a second spare wheel and snow chains, and the drivers hold a license for the vehicle class, a current medical fitness certificate (psicofísico) and workers' compensation insurance (ART).

How do you operate above 4,000 m, where there is no cell coverage?

We carry GPS on the units in service, and on high-altitude jobs a satellite phone and Starlink satellite internet travel on board, so the trip does not depend on the cell network.

What happens if one of our units is stranded in the Puna?

We go up with our own mechanics, welding equipment and generator, and with the flatbed tow truck when the vehicle has to be brought down. A unit stopped up there cannot wait for someone to improvise a rescue, so the recovery is planned as a trip of its own.
