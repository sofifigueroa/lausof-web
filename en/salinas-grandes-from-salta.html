How to get there

How to get from Salta to Salinas Grandes

Salta to Salinas Grandes is about 255 km (158 miles) and 4 h 10 of driving one way, via Purmamarca and the Cuesta de Lipán, so a return day trip is about 510 km. There is no direct bus from Salta; most visitors go on a shared tour or with a private transfer.

OptionWhat to know
Distance and timeAbout 255 km and 4 h 10 one way, without stops. From the airport, about 258 km and 4 h 20.
RoadHighway to San Salvador de Jujuy and RN 9 to Purmamarca; from there, 66 km on RN 52, which climbs the Cuesta de Lipán to about 4,170 m (13,700 ft). Paved.

The route

Road, distance and time from Salta

Salinas Grandes is a salt flat of about 212 sq km shared by Jujuy and Salta, at an average altitude of 3,450 m. From Purmamarca, RN 52 climbs the Cuesta de Lipán to about 4,170 m and comes down onto the salt flat.

LegRoad, approximate distance and drive time, altitude
Salta city to PurmamarcaAbout 188 km and 2 h 50 min on the toll highway through San Salvador de Jujuy (RN 9, RN 34 and RN 66), then north on RN 9 to Purmamarca, at 2,324 m.
Purmamarca to Salinas GrandesAbout 66 km and 1 h 25 min west on RN 52, a paved road. The highest point is the pass at the top of the Cuesta de Lipán, at about 4,170 m.
Salta city to Salinas GrandesAbout 255 km and 4 h 10 min one way, so about 510 km and more than eight hours of driving for the round trip, before any stop.
Salta airport (SLA) to Salinas GrandesAbout 258 km and 4 h 20 min one way: the airport is about 11 km from the city center.

Distances and times are Google Maps estimates read on October 10, 2026, without stops. The 66 km from Purmamarca, the 4,170 m pass, the 3,450 m altitude and the 212 sq km are those published by Argentina's national tourism board.

Altitude

From 1,200 m to 4,170 m in one morning

This is the highest of the classic day trips out of Salta. The city is at about 1,200 m, Purmamarca at 2,324 m, the Cuesta de Lipán pass at about 4,170 m and the salt flat at about 3,450 m. Most of the climb comes in the hour after Purmamarca.

  • The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) say that above about 2,440 m (8,000 ft) travelers may feel altitude illness. Going up for the day and sleeping lower is one of the patterns the CDC suggests, and it is what a day trip from Salta or from Purmamarca does.
  • The usual signs are headache, tiredness, loss of appetite and nausea. Someone whose symptoms get worse should go lower: on this route, that means driving back down towards Purmamarca.
  • The CDC also advises no alcohol and no heavy exercise in the first 48 hours at altitude.
  • Travelers with heart or lung conditions, and pregnant travelers, should talk to a doctor before the trip.

We take the climb at an easy pace and stop when the group needs to. If someone is not doing well, we turn back.

Planning

A long day, or a night in Purmamarca

Day trip from Salta. Salta, Purmamarca, Salinas Grandes and back is a full day: more than eight hours of driving before the stops. It works with an early start.

With a night in Purmamarca. Splitting the trip leaves time for the village and for the salt flat without hurry. Argentina's tourism board suggests staying at the salt flat until the end of the day for the sunset, which only makes sense if the group sleeps in Purmamarca, not in Salta.

Combined with the Quebrada. Purmamarca is also the door to Tilcara and Humahuaca: see Salta to Purmamarca, Tilcara and Humahuaca for a trip of two or three days.

What to bring. The tourism board recommends warm clothes, sunglasses and a hat or cap: the climate is arid, with a wide gap between day and night temperatures, and the white salt reflects the sun. December to March is the rainy season.

Frequently asked questions

What groups ask about Salinas Grandes

Can we do Salinas Grandes in one day from Salta?

Yes. It is a long day: about 510 km round trip and more than eight hours of driving before the stops (Google Maps estimate). An early start leaves time at Purmamarca and on the salt flat.

Which way do you go?

Through Jujuy: the highway to San Salvador de Jujuy, RN 9 north to Purmamarca, and RN 52 over the Cuesta de Lipán to the salt flat. It is paved the whole way.

Can we go through San Antonio de los Cobres instead?

Yes, on request. The salt flat can also be reached from the Salta side, through San Antonio de los Cobres, a town at about 3,775 m in the Puna of Salta. It is a higher and longer day; this page describes the route through Purmamarca. Ask for either when you write.

How high does it get, and is that a problem?

The pass reaches about 4,170 m and the salt flat sits at about 3,450 m. Most people feel the altitude; headache and tiredness are common. Going up for the day and sleeping lower is the pattern the CDC suggests. If anyone has a heart or lung condition, or is pregnant, they should ask a doctor first.

Can you collect us at Salta airport and go to the salt flat on the way to Purmamarca?

It is possible, but it means going from the plane to more than 4,000 m in a few hours. Many travelers prefer a first night in Purmamarca (2,324 m) and the salt flat the next morning. Tell us your flight and we plan it with you.

Is this a guided tour?

It can be. It is a private vehicle with a professional driver who knows the road. If you want a licensed tour guide, for the whole trip or at a particular site, we can arrange one: mention it when you ask for the quote.

What should we bring?

The tourism board recommends warm clothes, sunglasses and a hat or cap: the climate is arid, with a wide gap between day and night temperatures, and the white salt reflects the sun.

Do the drivers speak English?

Drivers work in Spanish; an English-speaking driver can be arranged on request, subject to availability.

How much does it cost?

The price is for the whole vehicle and depends on the route, the dates, the number of passengers and the vehicle the trip needs. Send those and we send you the price.
