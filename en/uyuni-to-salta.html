How to get there

How to get from Uyuni to Salta

Uyuni to Salta is about 690 km (430 miles): some 290 km of paved mountain road through Atocha and Tupiza to Villazón, the international bridge to La Quiaca, and about 400 km on Ruta 9 down to Salta. Driving time without stops is close to 9 and a half hours, plus the border, so we recommend two days. There is no direct bus: you can combine buses or the train with crossing the bridge on foot, or take a private transfer.

The road

Uyuni to Salta, leg by leg

It is about 690 km. On the Bolivian side, the Uyuni–Atocha–Tupiza road is a paved mountain road, completed in 2023, that runs on to Villazón. We cross the international bridge into La Quiaca and drive down Ruta 9, across the Jujuy plateau, through Humahuaca and the Quebrada, to Salta.

In this direction the trip goes downhill: Uyuni sits at about 3,650 m (12,000 ft), La Quiaca at about 3,400 m (11,200 ft) and Salta at about 1,200 m (3,900 ft). We recommend two days, with a night in Tupiza or the Quebrada; if your group prefers, we do it in one long day.

From Tupiza or Villazón

If your Bolivia trip ends in Tupiza or Villazón

Many travelers finish their tour of southern Bolivia in Tupiza or make their own way to the border. From there, the trip to Salta fits in one day.

  • From Tupiza: about 490 km and close to 7 hours of driving without stops, plus the border. We pick you up at your lodging.
  • From Villazón or La Quiaca: about 400 km and a little over 5 hours of driving to Salta. We pick you up in Villazón or, if you prefer to walk across the bridge, on the Argentine side in La Quiaca.
  • With stops: Humahuaca, Tilcara and Purmamarca are on the road. If you want a night in the Quebrada and to continue the next day, we plan it.

The border

The Villazón–La Quiaca crossing, heading into Argentina

It is the most direct crossing between southern Bolivia and Salta, on the same Ruta 9, and unlike the passes into Chile it does not depend on snow. You exit Bolivia in Villazón, cross the bridge and enter Argentina in La Quiaca: immigration, customs and the Argentine food and agriculture inspection (SENASA).

Official sources do not agree on the hours. On Nov 3, 2026, Argentina's page for the crossing listed 7:00 to midnight every day, Argentina time, while another government page says immigration is open 24 hours. Bolivia is one hour behind: 7:00 in La Quiaca is 6:00 in Villazón. We check the hours the day before and reach the border with time to spare.

Road blockades in Bolivia. Roads in Bolivia can be closed by blockades. The Bolivian Roads Administration (ABC) publishes the state of the national road network on its road-status map: we check it before leaving, and if the road is cut we tell you and reschedule with you.

Live status: La Quiaca–Villazón crossing on argentina.gob.ar (Border Police, National Roads and Weather Service) and the ABC road-status map. Checked Nov 3, 2026.

Documents and food rules

What each passenger needs to enter Argentina

  • Most travelers from outside South America: a valid passport. Depending on nationality, a tourist visa may be needed, issued by an Argentine consulate before the trip.
  • Argentine citizens: national ID card (DNI) or passport.
  • Citizens of Mercosur and associated countries (for example Bolivia, Chile, Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay, Peru, Colombia, Ecuador): the national ID card recognized under the Mercosur agreement, or a passport, unless their nationality needs a visa.
  • Minors crossing without both parents: Bolivia and Argentina each have their own rules. Ask a consulate about your case before the trip.

Argentine food rules (SENASA). On entering Argentina, SENASA checks the food you carry and can confiscate what is not allowed. Fresh fruit and vegetables, meat of any kind, cured sausages, honey, homemade food and unlabeled food are not allowed in. Factory-packed products such as canned food, jams, roasted or salted nuts and some dairy products are allowed in amounts for personal use. Our advice: bring no fresh food.

Official sources, checked Nov 3, 2026: Mercosur travel documents (Argentine immigration), tourist visa (Argentine Foreign Ministry), travel authorization for minors (Argentine immigration) and entering Argentina (SENASA). Rules change: confirm yours with your consulate before you travel.

Frequently asked questions

What travelers ask before heading from Uyuni to Salta

Can Uyuni to Salta be done in one day?

It can, but it is long: about 690 km and close to 9 and a half hours of driving, plus the border. We recommend two days, with a night in Tupiza or the Quebrada de Humahuaca. From Tupiza or Villazón, the trip fits in one day.

Is there a direct bus from Uyuni to Salta?

There is no direct service. You combine a bus or the train to Villazón, a walk across the bridge and a bus from La Quiaca to Salta. A private transfer does the whole trip in the same vehicle, from your lodging.

What if there is a road blockade in Bolivia?

We check the Bolivian Roads Administration map before leaving. If the road is cut, we do not risk leaving you stranded: we let you know and reschedule with you.

What food can I bring into Argentina?

The easiest choice is to bring no fresh food. SENASA does not allow fresh fruit and vegetables, meat, cured sausages, honey or homemade food, and can confiscate them at the border. Factory-packed products such as canned food or jams are allowed in amounts for personal use.

Do I need a visa for Argentina?

It depends on your nationality. Citizens of Mercosur and associated countries enter with their ID card. Everyone else enters with a passport, and some nationalities need a tourist visa from an Argentine consulate before the trip.

How much does it cost?

The price depends on the date, the number of travelers, where we pick you up, the number of days and the vehicle the group needs. Send us those details and we reply with a price.
