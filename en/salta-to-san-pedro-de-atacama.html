The road

Salta to San Pedro, leg by leg

It is about 590 km (370 miles), paved all the way. We leave on Ruta 9 toward Jujuy and Purmamarca, climb Ruta 52 onto the high plateau and cross into Chile at Jama. On the Chilean side, Ruta 27 CH drops down to San Pedro de Atacama.

Pure driving time is close to 8 hours. In practice it is a full day: add your stops, the border check and the slower pace the altitude calls for. We leave early so the crossing happens in daylight.

The border

The Jama Pass: opening hours and closures

Jama is an integrated border complex on the Argentine side, where both countries work in the same building. You exit Argentina and enter Chile in a single stop: immigration, customs and the Chilean agricultural inspection (SAG).

The two governments publish different hours, and the hours change with the season. On Oct 10, 2026, Argentina's official page listed 9:00 to 19:15 and Chile's listed 8:00 to 20:00, Chilean time. We plan the crossing in daylight with time to spare and check the hours the day before departure.

The pass closes for snow and blizzards, in winter and in summer too. In August 2026 it was closed from the 11th for snowfall, whiteout winds and sub-zero temperatures, with stretches of Ruta 27 CH impassable. There were closures in January as well, during the Altiplano summer storms. If the pass is closed on your travel day, we tell you before we leave and move the date with you.

Live status: Jama Pass on argentina.gob.ar (Border Police, National Roads and Weather Service) and the daily status of Chile's border complexes. August 2026 closure: El Tribuno de Jujuy, Aug 22, 2026. Checked Oct 10, 2026.

Documents and food rules

What each passenger needs

  • Most travelers from outside South America: a valid passport. Chile admits tourists for up to 90 days. Nationals of some countries need a prior authorization or visa from a Chilean consulate before the trip; the Chilean Foreign Ministry publishes the list.
  • Argentine citizens: national ID card (DNI) or passport.
  • Citizens of Mercosur and associated countries (for example Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay, Bolivia, Peru, Colombia, Ecuador): the national ID card recognized under the Mercosur agreement, or a passport, unless their nationality needs a visa.
  • On entering Chile, the Chilean police (PDI) record your stay on the Tarjeta Única Migratoria. Keep it until you leave the country.
  • Minors leaving Argentina may need a travel authorization, depending on who travels with them. Argentina's immigration office explains the cases.

Chilean food rules (SAG). Chilean law requires you to declare, on a sworn form, every product of plant or animal origin you carry on you, in your luggage or in the vehicle. Fresh fruit is not allowed in, and fruit and vegetables, dairy, honey and meat, fresh or processed, are among the most closely checked items. Not declaring is fined. Our advice: carry no fresh food, and declare any food at all, even if it is packaged.

Official sources, checked Oct 10, 2026: tourist stays in Chile (Chilean National Migration Service), visas to enter Chile (Chilean Foreign Ministry), Mercosur travel documents and travel authorization for minors (Argentine immigration), and the SAG sworn declaration. Rules change: confirm yours with your consulate before you travel.

Stops and return

What you can add to the trip

This is a private transfer, so the stops are yours to choose. The ones travelers ask for most on this road are the Hill of Seven Colors in Purmamarca, the Cuesta de Lipán viewpoint, Salinas Grandes and the village of Susques. Name the ones you want when you ask for a price and we build them into the schedule.

  • One way: we drop you at your lodging in San Pedro de Atacama.
  • Return: we pick you up in San Pedro on the agreed date and drive you back to Salta, or to Salta or Jujuy airport.
  • Combined trips: San Pedro as part of a longer route through Salta and Jujuy, or together with Bolivia, planned on request.

A note on time zones: from September to April mainland Chile is on summer time and shares Argentina's clock; the rest of the year Chile is one hour behind. We account for it when setting the crossing and pickup times.

Frequently asked questions

What travelers ask before crossing to Chile

How long does Salta to San Pedro de Atacama take?

Driving time is close to 8 hours for about 590 km. With stops and the border check at Jama it is a full day, so we leave early to cross in daylight.

What happens if the Jama Pass is closed?

We check the pass the day before and again before leaving. If it is closed for snow or wind, we do not set off: we let you know and find the nearest date when the pass is open.

Will the altitude affect me?

The road goes above 4,000 m (13,000 ft) at the Cuesta de Lipán and at Jama, which sits at 4,200 m. Many people feel it. We climb steadily and stop along the way; drink water and eat light. If you have a medical condition, ask your doctor before the trip.

Can I bring food across?

The easiest choice is to carry no fresh food. Chile does not allow fresh fruit and checks vegetables, dairy, honey and meat. Any food of plant or animal origin goes on the SAG sworn declaration, and failing to declare it is fined.

Do I need a visa for Chile?

It depends on your nationality. Argentines and citizens of Mercosur and associated countries enter with their ID card. Everyone else enters with a passport, and some nationalities need a prior authorization from a Chilean consulate. Check the Chilean Foreign Ministry's visa page before you travel.

Do you publish prices?

No. The price depends on the date, the number of travelers, one way or return, and the vehicle the group needs. Send us those details and we reply with a price.
