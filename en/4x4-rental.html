Driving here from abroad

License, documents and border crossings

What the Argentine, Chilean and Bolivian authorities ask of someone driving a rented vehicle, according to their own official pages.

Driver's license

A license issued by a country that is party to the Geneva (1949) or Vienna (1968) road traffic conventions is valid in Argentina for up to one year from your entry. If your country does not issue a national license, you need an international driving permit, valid for one year from its date of issue. Source: argentina.gob.ar

International Driving Permit

We recommend bringing one even when your license is valid: it does not replace your license, it goes with it, and it makes any roadside check easier. It is issued in your home country before you travel.

Identity document

Your passport with the Argentine entry stamp, or an ID card valid for travel within MERCOSUR.

Crossing into Chile

To leave Argentina in a rented vehicle, Argentine customs asks for a current license and the MERCOSUR circulation authorization (ACM) issued by the rental company for the chosen pass. On entry, Chile requires personal accident insurance for foreign-plated vehicles and admits the vehicle for up to 90 days. Source: ARCA · Chilean customs

Crossing into Bolivia

Argentine customs asks for the vehicle's temporary exit form and the owner's authorization for it to leave the country. Source: ARCA
